# Vaccination Guide

## What is Vaccination?
Vaccination is the administration of a vaccine to help the immune system develop protection from a disease. Vaccines contain a piece that resembles a disease-causing microorganism and are made from weakened or killed forms of the microbe.

## Why Vaccination Matters
Vaccination is one of the most cost-effective health interventions available. It prevents illness, disability, and death from vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles, polio, tetanus, and influenza.

## How Vaccines Work
When you receive a vaccine, your immune system learns to recognize and fight the specific virus or bacteria. If you are later exposed to the disease, your body can respond quickly to prevent infection or reduce its severity.

## Common Vaccines
- **Influenza (Flu)**: Recommended annually for everyone aged 6 months and older
- **COVID-19**: Protects against severe illness, hospitalization, and death
- **MMR (Measles, Mumps, Rubella)**: Given in childhood, provides lifelong protection
- **Tetanus/Diphtheria/Pertussis (Tdap)**: Booster needed every 10 years
- **Hepatitis B**: Series of 2-3 doses for children and at-risk adults

## Community Immunity
When a high percentage of the population is vaccinated, it becomes difficult for diseases to spread. This protects people who cannot be vaccinated, including infants, elderly individuals, and those with weakened immune systems.

## Where to Get Vaccinated
- Local vaccination centers
- Community health clinics
- Pharmacies
- Public health departments
- Many vaccines are available at no cost through public health programs

## Safety
Vaccines undergo rigorous testing before approval and are continuously monitored for safety. Side effects are typically mild and temporary, such as soreness at the injection site or low-grade fever.
